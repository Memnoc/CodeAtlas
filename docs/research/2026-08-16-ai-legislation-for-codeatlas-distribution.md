## The system under audit (facts relied on)

CodeAtlas (github.com/Memnoc/CodeAtlas, public, MIT-licensed, owner Memnoc/Matteo Stara) is a local developer tool written in Rust. Facts this analysis relies on, verified in-repo and taken as given:

1. Scanning is deterministic (tree-sitter parsers); the interactive map is served only on 127.0.0.1 loopback; a redacted static share artifact can be exported.
2. No model weights are shipped; no local inference is performed.
3. Two optional features call Anthropic's Claude API **using the operator's own credentials** (env key or their logged-in CLI): **enrich** (prose summaries of code, stored locally in an annotation store recording provider/model/UTC-date of the last writing run) and **ask** (Q&A over the map; bounded map-derived context, never file contents).
4. AI-generated prose is visibly badged in the dashboard where it renders; the share export redacts it entirely.
5. No telemetry, no accounts, no data collection by the maker. A "sealed" build variant compiles all egress out.
6. Distribution: public source on GitHub; first binary release (GitHub Releases, 8 Linux/macOS binaries, SHA-256 checksums, GitHub build-provenance attestation) is imminent as of 2026-08-16. Distribution is worldwide by default. Maker's jurisdiction unconfirmed (likely UK).
7. The tool's output influences no decisions about people; no biometrics; no personal-data processing by design; users are software developers.

# Pack 1 — EU AI Act (Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, as amended by Regulation (EU) 2026/1744)

## 1.1 Regulated object — is CodeAtlas an "AI system"? (Q1)

**Art 3(1)** (verbatim, official OJ text): *"'AI system' means a machine-based system that is designed to operate with varying levels of autonomy and that may exhibit adaptiveness after deployment, and that, for explicit or implicit objectives, infers, from the input it receives, how to generate outputs such as predictions, content, recommendations, or decisions that can influence physical or virtual environments."*

**Sealed / no-key configuration: not an AI system.** The scanning pipeline is deterministic tree-sitter parsing and rendering — rules defined solely by humans, no inference component of any kind. The Commission Guidelines on the AI-system definition (C(2025) 924 final, 6 Feb 2025) put such software squarely out of scope: para 40 (quoting Recital 12: the definition *"should not cover systems that are based on the rules defined solely by natural persons to automatically execute operations"*), paras 46–47 ("basic data processing" — systems following "predefined, explicit instructions or operations", including systems "solely intended for descriptive analysis … and visualisation"), para 48 (classical heuristics). Status of the guidelines checked: approved by the Commission 6 Feb 2025; **non-binding** by their own terms (para 7: *"The Guidelines are not binding. Any authoritative interpretation of the AI Act may ultimately only be given by the Court of Justice of the European Union"*).

**With-key configuration: the text does not clearly resolve it.** CodeAtlas itself still performs no inference; the inference happens server-side at Anthropic, under a contract between the *operator* and Anthropic. Points pulling toward "AI system": Recital 97 (*"Although AI models are essential components of AI systems, they do not constitute AI systems on their own. AI models require the addition of further components, such as for example a user interface, to become AI systems"*) — CodeAtlas supplies exactly such further components (UI, prompt construction, bounded context); and Art 3(68) defines a *"downstream provider"* as a provider of an AI system *"which integrates an AI model, regardless of whether the AI model is provided by themselves and vertically integrated or provided by another entity based on contractual relations."* Points pulling the other way: the artifact actually distributed contains no model and no access to one — the AI features are inert until the operator supplies their own credentials, and the "contractual relations" of Art 3(68) here run operator→Anthropic, not maker→Anthropic. **The C(2025) 924 guidelines were checked for this scenario and are silent: they contain no treatment of systems that merely route requests to an external third-party model under the user's own credentials** (verified by full-text search of the guidelines PDF for API/integration/routing passages). → **Counsel question CQ1.**

Anthropic's Claude is, separately, a general-purpose AI model (Art 3(63)) whose provider is Anthropic; a Claude-based service is a general-purpose AI system (Art 3(66)). Nothing about CodeAtlas changes Anthropic's own obligations.

## 1.2 Territorial scope

**Art 2(1)(a):** the Regulation applies to *"providers placing on the market or putting into service AI systems … in the Union, irrespective of whether those providers are established or located within the Union or in a third country."* Worldwide-by-default GitHub distribution reaches the Union, so territorial scope is satisfied **if and only if** there is (i) an AI system and (ii) a "placing on the market" — both contested below. Art 2(1)(c) (third-country providers where the *output* is used in the Union) adds nothing beyond (a) here.

## 1.3 The maker's legal role on first GitHub Release (Q2)

- **Provider (Art 3(3))** (verbatim): *"a natural or legal person … that develops an AI system or a general-purpose AI model or that has an AI system or a general-purpose AI model developed and places it on the market or puts the AI system into service under its own name or trademark, whether for payment or free of charge."* If CodeAtlas-with-key is an AI system and the release is a "placing on the market", the maker is its **provider** (develops it, releases under his own name, free of charge is expressly covered). He is not a **distributor** (Art 3(7): *"a natural or legal person in the supply chain, other than the provider or the importer, that makes an AI system available on the Union market"*) — of CodeAtlas he would be the provider, and he does not make *Claude* available (operators bring their own access).
- **"Placing on the market" (Art 3(9)):** *"the first making available of an AI system or a general-purpose AI model on the Union market"*; **"making available" (Art 3(10)):** *"the supply of an AI system or a general-purpose AI model for distribution or use on the Union market **in the course of a commercial activity**, whether in return for payment or free of charge."* The commercial-activity element is the gateway. A free, MIT-licensed release by an individual, with no payment, no paid support, no monetisation of any kind, is arguably not supply "in the course of a commercial activity" at all. Recital 103 supports this direction for open channels: AI components provided against a price or otherwise monetised (including via paid technical support or a software platform, or use of personal data beyond security/compatibility) lose the open-source exceptions, but *"[t]he fact of making AI components available through open repositories should not, in itself, constitute a monetisation."* The Act nowhere defines "commercial activity", and the recital addresses monetisation rather than the Art 3(10) element directly. The Commission's Blue Guide on EU product rules (Commission notice 2022/C 247/01, §2.2 — interpretive guidance for the New Legislative Framework whose "making available" definition Art 3(10) mirrors; verified at `http://publications.europa.eu/resource/celex/52022XC0629%2804%29`) reads the element the same way: *"Commercial activity is understood as providing goods in a business related context. … This can only be appreciated on a case by case basis taking into account the regularity of the supplies, the characteristics of the product, the intentions of the supplier, etc. In principle, occasional supplies by charities or hobbyists should not be considered as taking place in a business related context."* A hobbyist/side-project release with zero monetisation sits on the favourable side of that line, but the Blue Guide is soft law, is written for tangible products, and "case by case" is exactly what it says. → **Counsel question CQ2.** Note the analysis is identical for source distribution and for the binary GitHub Release: both are supply free of charge through an open repository.

## 1.4 Classification — which carve-outs from the open-source exemption could engage? (Q3)

**Art 2(12)** (verbatim): *"This Regulation does not apply to AI systems released under free and open-source licences, unless they are placed on the market or put into service as high-risk AI systems or as an AI system that falls under Article 5 or 50."* MIT is a free and open-source licence in the sense described by Recital 102 (free access, use, modification, redistribution, attribution conditions permitted). Recitals 102–104 frame the exemption; Recital 103 sets its monetisation limits (none present on the given facts); Recital 89 separately shields third parties supplying FOSS tools/components from value-chain duties toward downstream providers (mirrored in Art 25(4), last sentence).

Checking each carve-out against the facts:

- **Art 5 (prohibited practices)** — none engages. The Art 5(1) list (verified in the official text) covers subliminal/manipulative techniques, exploitation of vulnerabilities, social scoring, criminal risk-prediction on profiling, untargeted facial-image scraping, emotion inference at work/school, biometric categorisation, real-time remote biometric identification — plus, added by Regulation 2026/1744 (applying from 2 Dec 2026), generation/manipulation of non-consensual intimate material and CSAM (new Art 5(1) points (ba), (bb)). A code-mapping developer tool does none of these.
- **High-risk** — none engages. Art 6(1)/Annex I (regulated-product safety components): no. Art 6(2)/Annex III areas (verified headings: biometrics; critical infrastructure; education; employment; essential services; law enforcement; migration/asylum/border; administration of justice and democratic processes): a code-mapping developer tool falls in none, and the facts stipulate its output influences no decisions about people.
- **Art 50 (transparency)** — this is the only live carve-out. Analysed next.

## 1.5 Article 50, subsection by subsection (Q4)

All quotations verbatim from the official OJ text of Art 50 (Chapter IV), as in force 2026-08-16 (Regulation 2026/1744 replaced only Art 50(7), the codes-of-practice mechanism).

- **Art 50(1) — interaction transparency.** *"Providers shall ensure that AI systems intended to interact directly with natural persons are designed and developed in such a way that the natural persons concerned are informed that they are interacting with an AI system, **unless this is obvious** from the point of view of a natural person who is reasonably well-informed, observant and circumspect, taking into account the circumstances and the context of use."* If the with-key configuration is an AI system at all (CQ1), the "ask" feature is plausibly "intended to interact directly with natural persons". Two independent outs, both on the face of the text: (i) the feature is labelled as asking Claude, which is exactly the design measure Art 50(1) requires — so the obligation, if borne, is **already satisfied**; (ii) the "obvious" exception has unusual force here, since the natural person concerned personally supplied the Anthropic credentials and invoked the feature. Bearer: Art 50(1) binds the *provider of the AI system that interacts*; whether that is the maker (if CodeAtlas is the system) and/or Anthropic (for the underlying GPAI system) is the allocation limb of **CQ1** — but on either allocation the labelling satisfies it, so nothing further attaches in practice. Timing note: Art 50(5) requires the information *"at the latest at the time of the first interaction"* — the current badge/labelling placement should be checked against that (it renders where the prose renders, and the ask flow is explicit; see Evidence needed).
- **Art 50(2) — machine-readable marking of synthetic content.** *"Providers of AI systems, including general-purpose AI systems, generating synthetic audio, image, video or text content, shall ensure that the outputs of the AI system are marked in a machine-readable format and detectable as artificially generated or manipulated,"* with proportionality qualifiers (*"as far as this is technically feasible"*, state of the art, cost), and an exception for systems performing *"an assistive function for standard editing"* or not substantially altering deployer-provided input (the enrich summaries are generated prose, so that exception does not fit). The enrich output **is** synthetic text. Two questions the text does not clearly resolve: **(a) whose obligation** — the generating model/system is Anthropic's; Recital 133 says the marking techniques *"can be implemented at the level of the AI system or at the level of the AI model, including general-purpose AI models generating content, thereby facilitating fulfilment of this obligation by the downstream provider of the AI system"* — language that contemplates a downstream system provider as an obligation-bearer, while presupposing model-level solutions it can rely on; and **(b) what suffices** — whether the annotation store's recorded provider/model/UTC-date plus visible badging amounts to output *"marked in a machine-readable format and detectable as artificially generated"*. The store's provenance record is machine-readable and attached to every AI-written annotation, which is at minimum most of the way there; whether marking must travel *with the text itself* (metadata/watermark-style) is not answered by the text, and the Art 50(7) codes of practice that would answer it are still being drawn up. → **Counsel question CQ3.** Mitigations already in the design: the share export **redacts AI prose entirely**, so no synthetic content leaves the operator's machine via the one distribution channel the tool provides.
- **Art 50(3)** — emotion recognition / biometric categorisation: inapplicable on the facts (none present).
- **Art 50(4), first subparagraph** — deep fakes (image/audio/video): inapplicable; CodeAtlas generates no image/audio/video content.
- **Art 50(4), second subparagraph** — *"**Deployers** of an AI system that generates or manipulates text which is **published with the purpose of informing the public on matters of public interest** shall disclose that the text has been artificially generated…"* Plainly inapplicable to the maker, on the text, twice over: (i) it binds *deployers* (Art 3(4): a person *"using an AI system under its authority"* — that is the operator, not the maker; and the maker's own use of his tool is not what is being audited); (ii) locally stored code summaries for a developer's own understanding are not "published with the purpose of informing the public on matters of public interest", and the only publishable artifact (share export) strips the AI text entirely.

## 1.6 GPAI obligations, Chapter V (Q5)

Inapplicable. Chapter V obligations (Arts 51–55) attach to *"providers of general-purpose AI models"* (Art 53(1)); Art 3(63) defines a GPAI model by generality, capability breadth and training scale. The maker distributes no model of any kind (fact 2). Even if the maker were one day a "downstream provider" (Art 3(68)) of an AI *system*, Chapter V duties would still sit with the *model* provider (Anthropic), not with him. The FOSS-model exceptions in Art 53(2) are therefore not even needed.

## 1.7 Applicable dates as of 2026-08-16, including what actually happened to "stop the clock" (Q6)

**Art 113** (original, verbatim structure): entry into force 1 Aug 2024; *"It shall apply from 2 August 2026"*, except (a) Chapters I–II from 2 Feb 2025; (b) Chapter III Section 4, Chapter V, Chapter VII, Chapter XII and Art 78 from 2 Aug 2025 (except Art 101); (c) Art 6(1) and corresponding obligations from 2 Aug 2027.

**What actually happened in 2025/2026 (from the official OJ text, not commentary):** the Commission's November 2025 "Digital Omnibus on AI" proposal was adopted as **Regulation (EU) 2026/1744 of 8 July 2026** (OJ 24.7.2026), entering into force on the third day following publication, i.e. **27 July 2026**. Its AI Act amendments relevant here, verified against the amending text:

- **Art 113(3)(c) replaced:** Chapter III Sections 1–3 (high-risk requirements) now apply from **2 December 2027** for Art 6(2)/Annex III systems and **2 August 2028** for Art 6(1)/Annex I systems.
- **Art 113(3)(a) replaced:** Chapters I–II still apply from 2 Feb 2025, except the newly added prohibitions (Art 5(1)(ba), (bb) and Art 5(1a), (1b) — NCII/CSAM generation) which apply from **2 December 2026**.
- **Art 50 was NOT postponed.** The general application date of 2 August 2026 stands; **Art 50 has been applicable law since 2 Aug 2026**, i.e. for two weeks as of this note.
- **New Art 111(4)** (transitional, verbatim): *"Providers of AI systems, including general-purpose AI systems, generating synthetic audio, image, video or text content, that have been placed on the market before 2 August 2026 shall take the necessary steps in order to comply with Article 50(2) by 2 December 2026."* Note the direction of this: it is a grace period **only for systems placed on the market before 2 Aug 2026**. A first release cut in the coming weeks (after 2 Aug 2026) gets **no** transitional period — if Art 50(2) attaches, it attaches from day one.
- Art 50(7) was replaced (codes of practice on detection/marking/labelling now assessed by Commission with Board opinion; the implementing-act empowerment was removed). No change to Art 2(12), Art 3(1) or the substance of Art 50(1)–(6).

So, current state on 2026-08-16: prohibitions (Art 5, pre-omnibus list) — in application since 2 Feb 2025; GPAI (Chapter V) — since 2 Aug 2025; **the bulk of the Act including Art 50 — in application since 2 Aug 2026**; high-risk Chapter III Sections 1–3 — deferred to Dec 2027 / Aug 2028; new Art 5 prohibitions — 2 Dec 2026.

## 1.8 Net for Pack 1 — obligation or exemption (Q7)

Layered, in order of how the questions actually gate each other:

1. **Sealed / no-key builds:** not an AI system (Art 3(1); C(2025) 924 paras 40, 46–48). The Act does not apply. Firm.
2. **With-key build:** whether it is an AI system at all is unresolved (CQ1). If it is not, the Act does not apply and the analysis ends.
3. Even if it is an AI system, a free, unmonetised, open-repository release is arguably not "making available … in the course of a commercial activity" (Art 3(10); Recital 103), hence no "placing on the market" (Art 3(9)) and no scope hook under Art 2(1)(a) (CQ2).
4. Even if placed on the market, **Art 2(12)** exempts the MIT-licensed release from the entire Regulation **except** the Art 5 / high-risk / Art 50 carve-outs; Art 5 and high-risk demonstrably do not engage on the facts (§1.4).
5. The only obligation that could concretely attach is **Art 50**, live since 2 Aug 2026, with no transitional period for a post-2 Aug 2026 first release: **Art 50(1)** is satisfied by the existing labelling/badging design even if borne by the maker; **Art 50(2)** is the single provision with real implementation content, and both its allocation (Anthropic vs maker) and its satisfaction standard (whether badging + machine-readable provider/model/date provenance suffices) are open (CQ3). **Art 50(3)–(4)** are inapplicable on the text.

**Practical risk posture** (evidence, not advice): the exposure surface is Art 50(2) only, in the with-key configuration only, and only if CQ1 and CQ2 both resolve against the maker. The low-cost hardening that would moot CQ3's second limb regardless of allocation: ensure the machine-readable provenance record is inseparably attached to each stored AI-generated annotation (it already records provider/model/UTC-date) and keep the existing badging and export-redaction behaviour.

# Pack 2 — GDPR (Regulation (EU) 2016/679)

- **Regulated object:** *processing of personal data* (Art 2(1): the Regulation *"applies to the processing of personal data wholly or partly by automated means…"*). Distributing software is not, in itself, processing personal data.
- **Territorial scope:** Art 3 — moot if no processing by the maker exists (none does).
- **The maker's legal role:** neither **controller** nor **processor** for operators' use of the tool. Art 4(7) (verbatim): controller is the person who *"alone or jointly with others, **determines the purposes and means of the processing** of personal data"*; Art 4(8): processor is one who *"**processes personal data on behalf of** the controller."* With no telemetry, no accounts and no data collection (fact 5), the maker performs no processing operation whatsoever and determines the purposes and means of none. EDPB Guidelines 07/2020 (v2.0, 7 July 2021), para 76: the two conditions for processor status are *"a) being a separate entity in relation to the controller and b) processing personal data on the controller's behalf"* — condition (b) is absent. The same guidelines' controller analysis ties controllership to actual or legally-attributed determination of an actual processing operation (Part I, section 2; paras 20 ff.). CJEU framing, verified against the official text (Publications Office copy, `http://publications.europa.eu/resource/celex/62017CJ0040`): *Fashion ID*, C-40/17, ECLI:EU:C:2019:629 — a person *"cannot be considered to be a controller … in the context of operations that precede or are subsequent in the overall chain of processing for which that person does not determine either the purposes or the means"* (para 74), liability being *"limited to the operation or set of operations … in respect of which it actually determines the purposes and means"* (para 85). A maker who participates in no processing operation at all is a fortiori outside controllership.
- **Classification / dates / obligation:** GDPR fully in force throughout; **no obligation attaches to the maker qua software distributor**. Operators who run CodeAtlas over codebases containing personal data are themselves controllers of that processing (and of anything they send to Anthropic under their own API contract) — Art 4(7); their affair, not the maker's. The household exemption (Art 2(2)(c)) is not even needed for the maker's position, and Recital 18's last sentence (providers of "the means" for household processing) addresses entities that themselves process — inapposite to a supplier of locally-run software who never touches the data.
- **Evidence needed:** the no-telemetry / no-accounts property, verifiable in the open source; the sealed build as corroboration. If the maker ever adds telemetry, crash reporting or update pings, this pack must be redone from scratch.

**Bottom line (Pack 2):** distributing CodeAtlas makes the maker neither controller nor processor — Art 4(7)–(8) simply do not reach an entity that processes nothing.

# Pack 3 — United Kingdom (likely maker jurisdiction)

*(Findings verified against official sources — the Parliament Bills API backing bills.parliament.uk, legislation.gov.uk, gov.uk publications — citations inline. Where the Parliament web front-end returned HTTP 403 to automated retrieval, the official Bills API JSON for the same records was read directly.)*

- **Regulated object:** none exists. As of 2026-08-16 the UK has **no cross-sector AI statute**, in force or enacted-but-not-yet-in-force. The governing approach remains the non-statutory, principles-based, regulator-led framework of the March 2023 white paper *"AI regulation: a pro-innovation approach"* (DSIT, 29 Mar 2023 — `https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/ai-regulation-a-pro-innovation-approach`), confirmed by the 6 Feb 2024 government consultation response (*"We think a non-statutory approach currently offers critical adaptability"*; future binding requirements discussed only for developers of *"highly capable general-purpose AI"* — `https://www.gov.uk/government/consultations/ai-regulation-a-pro-innovation-approach-policy-proposals/outcome/a-pro-innovation-approach-to-ai-regulation-government-response`).
- **Bill status, from the official record:** the Artificial Intelligence (Regulation) Bill [HL] (Lord Holmes, private member's bill 3942) records a **sole stage: 1st reading, House of Lords, 4 March 2025**; `isAct: false`; confined to session 2024–26, which ended before the 13 May 2026 King's Speech opened the new session. Private members' bills do not carry over; it lapsed unenacted (`https://bills.parliament.uk/bills/3942`, data via `https://bills-api.parliament.uk/api/v1/Bills/3942` and `/Stages`). No government AI bill has ever been introduced: Bills API sweeps for "artificial"/"intelligence"/"frontier" return only three AI-titled private members' bills across all sessions, none an Act; the July 2024 King's Speech only *promised* to "seek to establish the appropriate legislation to place requirements on those working to develop the most powerful artificial intelligence models" (background briefing notes PDF, PM's Office), and the **13 May 2026 King's Speech programme contains no AI bill** (`https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6a18713db95db968c8f3bbfd/The_King_s_Speech_2026_-_background_briefing_notes.pdf`). A third-party claim that a "UK AI Safety Act" was introduced in late 2025 is contradicted by the official record (no such bill on the Bills API; no such Act on legislation.gov.uk) and is treated as false.
- **Closest enacted instruments, checked against their own scope text:**
  - **Data (Use and Access) Act 2025 (c. 18)** (Royal Assent 19 June 2025): amends UK data-protection law (s. 80/Sch. 6 reform automated-decision-making rules in UK GDPR/DPA 2018) and places AI-and-copyright reporting duties on the *Secretary of State* — nothing in it regulates distribution of AI software (`https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2025/18/contents`, `/section/80`).
  - **SI 2026/425** — Data Protection Act 2018 (Code of Practice on Artificial Intelligence and Automated Decision-Making) Regulations 2026, made 16 Apr 2026, in force 12 May 2026: requires the ICO to *prepare a code of practice* on good practice in **processing personal data** in AI development/use. A data-protection instrument aimed at controllers/processors; imposes nothing on a maker who processes no personal data (`https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2026/425/made`).
  - **Online Safety Act 2023 (c. 50)**: duties attach to "user-to-user services" and "search services" (Part 2, s. 3 — a service by which user-generated content "may be encountered by another user"). A loopback-only local tool serving its own operator is outside the service definitions (`https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2023/50/section/3`).
  - **Product Regulation and Metrology Act 2025 (c. 20)**: enabling Act; s. 1(7) defines "product" as "a tangible item that results from a method of production" — downloaded software is outside it; no relevant regulations made under it were found (`https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2025/20/section/1`).
- **The maker's legal role / obligation:** none arises from AI-specific law, because none exists; UK GDPR/DPA 2018 duties attach to whoever processes personal data (the operator, if anyone), mirroring Pack 2.
- **Applicable dates:** n/a — nothing to date.
- **Evidence needed:** confirmation of the maker's actual jurisdiction (currently "likely UK" — unconfirmed); a periodic re-check of the session-40 bill list, since a government frontier-AI bill has been repeatedly signalled (it would, on every official signal so far, target developers of the most powerful models, not tool distributors).

**Bottom line (Pack 3):** no UK statute in force or enacted as of 2026-08-16 imposes obligations on distributing this tool; the UK's approach remains non-statutory and regulator-led, and the only adjacent instruments are data-protection ones that bite on processors of personal data, which the maker is not.

## 4.1 Federal

- **Regulated object / obligation:** none. No generally applicable federal AI statute imposes obligations on distributing this tool as of 2026-08-16. ## 4.2 Colorado (SB24-205 line)

- **Applicable dates — what actually happened, from the signed acts on leg.colorado.gov:** (1) **SB24-205** (signed 17 May 2024) wrote all duties as "on and after February 1, 2026" (`https://leg.colorado.gov/bill_files/47770/download`); (2) **SB25B-004** (2025 special session, signed 28 Aug 2025) substituted **June 30, 2026** everywhere in §§ 6-1-1702, 6-1-1703 and 6-1-1704(1), changing no definitions (`https://leg.colorado.gov/bill_files/90530/download`); (3) **SB26-189** (signed **14 May 2026**) *repeals and reenacts* part 17 as an automated-decision-making-technology (ADMT) framework, **taking effect January 1, 2027** and applying to consequential decisions made on or after that date (`https://leg.colorado.gov/bill_files/116489/download`). **Textual interregnum flagged:** because the repeal-and-reenactment itself commences 1 Jan 2027, the old part 17 (operative "on and after June 30, 2026") arguably remains on the books on 2026-08-16; the Colorado AG has reportedly disclaimed enforcement pending rulemaking (April 2026 filing — located via secondary sources only, not docket-verified). The statutory text does not cleanly resolve the 30 June – 31 Dec 2026 window, so both regimes were analysed. → **Counsel question CQ5** (low materiality, see below).
- **Classification under either regime — the tool escapes on the definitions:** Old act (C.R.S. 6-1-1701 as enacted): §§ 6-1-1702/1703 duties attach **only** to "high-risk" systems — those that make or are "a substantial factor in making, a consequential decision" (§ 1701(9)(a)), "consequential decision" being confined to material effects on education, employment, financial services, essential government services, health care, housing, insurance or legal services (§ 1701(3)). A code-mapping tool makes none. The only non-high-risk duty, **§ 6-1-1704** (disclosure that a consumer "is interacting with an artificial intelligence system"), is triply blocked: (i) "developer"/"deployer" both require *"a person doing business in this state"* (§ 1701(6)–(7)) — a poor fit for a UK individual giving software away, though "doing business" is undefined; (ii) the shipped Rust tool does not itself "infer" (§ 1701(2)) — the inferring system is Anthropic's, on the operator's own credentials; and (iii) the ask feature's explicit Claude labelling both performs the disclosure and lands in the § 1704(2) exception for circumstances *"obvious to a reasonable person"*. The replacement regime (from 1 Jan 2027) is narrower still: new § 6-1-1701(2)(b) **expressly excludes** tools "used by an individual solely to summarize, organize, translate, draft, route, or present information for human review" and natural-language systems answering questions when not intended for consequential decisions; developer duties attach only where the ADMT was "marketed, advertised, configured, contracted, sold, or licensed to be used to materially influence a consequential decision" (new § 6-1-1702(3)).
- **Obligation:** none attaches under either regime.

# (b) Counsel questions

Named questions for qualified counsel. Each is something the primary text does not clearly resolve; none is resolved in this note.

**CQ1 — The bring-your-own-key boundary (EU AI Act Art 3(1), Recital 97, Art 3(68)).**
*Question:* Is CodeAtlas, in its with-key configuration, an "AI system" under Art 3(1) — and if so, is the maker its "provider" — when the shipped artifact contains no model, performs no inference, is inert without credentials, and the inference component is a third-party service under a contract between the operator and Anthropic? Includes the allocation limb: if it is an AI system, do Art 50(1)/(2) sit with the maker, with Anthropic (as provider of the underlying GPAI model/system), or with both?
*Why it is material:* it gates the entire EU pack — if the answer is no, the AI Act does not apply to any configuration and CQ2/CQ3 fall away.
*What turns on it:* whether any EU-law obligation at all can attach to the release. The Commission's own definitional guidelines (C(2025) 924 final) were checked and are silent on API-routing systems; Recital 97 and Art 3(68) point toward "system", the inert-without-credentials distribution model points away.

**CQ2 — "In the course of a commercial activity" (EU AI Act Art 3(9)–(10), Recital 103; Blue Guide 2022/C 247/01 §2.2).**
*Question:* Does a free, MIT-licensed, wholly unmonetised GitHub release (source and binaries) by an individual constitute "making available on the market … in the course of a commercial activity", i.e. a "placing on the market"? Sub-question: at what point would adjacent activity (paid support, sponsorship, donations, consulting linked to the tool, prominent professional self-promotion) tip it?
*Why it is material:* it is the scope gateway (Art 2(1)(a)); if there is no placing on the market, the Act does not reach the release even if CQ1 resolves to "AI system".
*What turns on it:* whether Art 50 duties can attach at all, and how much commercial activity the maker can ever add around CodeAtlas without re-opening the analysis.

**CQ3 — Art 50(2): bearer and satisfaction standard.**
*Question:* If Art 50 engages (CQ1+CQ2), (a) does the machine-readable-marking duty for Claude-generated summaries rest on the maker as downstream system provider, on Anthropic, or both (Recital 133's model-level-marking language cuts both ways); and (b) does the current design — visible badging plus a machine-readable annotation-store record of provider/model/UTC-date per writing run, with full redaction of AI prose from the only exportable artifact — satisfy "marked in a machine-readable format and detectable as artificially generated", or must the marking travel embedded in the text itself (watermark/metadata)?
*Why it is material:* Art 50(2) is the single obligation in this note with non-trivial implementation cost, it has been applicable law since 2 Aug 2026, and a post-2 Aug release gets no transitional period.
*What turns on it:* whether any engineering change is needed before or shortly after the first binary release; the Art 50(7) codes of practice now being drawn up will eventually answer (b) — worth tracking.

**CQ4 — California AB 2013: does "designs, codes, produces" reach an API-wrapper? (Cal. Civ. Code §§ 3110–3111.)**
*Question:* Is the maker a "developer" of a generative AI "system or service" made "publicly available to Californians" when CodeAtlas only wraps Claude under the operator's own key — given the statute has no wrapper, open-source, or BYO-key carve-out?
*Why it is material:* if yes, a training-data documentation duty attaches (operative since 1 Jan 2026, free distribution not exempt) that the maker is factually unable to satisfy for a model he did not train — the interpretive arguments against applicability are strong (the required disclosures are knowable only to the model trainer; the tool generates nothing without the operator's credentials) but they are arguments, not text.
*What turns on it:* whether US distribution needs a documentation page (even one that simply points at Anthropic's published training-data disclosures) or a rethink of default availability to Californians.

**CQ5 — Colorado: the 30 June – 31 Dec 2026 interregnum and "doing business in this state" (C.R.S. 6-1-1701 et seq.; SB25B-004; SB26-189).**
*Question:* (a) Is the original SB24-205 regime textually operative between 30 June 2026 and SB26-189's 1 Jan 2027 repeal-and-reenactment, and what weight does the AG's reported non-enforcement position carry? (b) Could a UK individual distributing free software worldwide be "doing business in" Colorado for § 6-1-1701(6)–(7)?
*Why it is material:* low — even on the worst reading, the only reachable duty (§ 6-1-1704 AI-interaction disclosure) is already performed by the Claude labelling and falls within the "obvious to a reasonable person" exception; carried for completeness because the interregnum is textually unresolved and the AG filing was not docket-verified.
*What turns on it:* nothing operational today; only whether the audit file needs the docket-verified filing as evidence.
